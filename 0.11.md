# Symbol-Metric Neutrality: Number Systems as Valuation-Agnostic Representations

**Author:** [Rowan Brad Quni-Gudzinas](mailto://rowan.quni@outlook.com)

## 1. Introduction

Consider the symbol string `10`. In the conventional reading — positional notation, base $b = 10$ — this represents the quantity $10 = 1 \cdot 10^1 + 0 \cdot 10^0$. Under the real (Archimedean) absolute value $|\cdot|$, the distance of this number from zero is $|10| = 10$.

But this is not the only possible reading. Under the $2$-adic absolute value, the distance from zero is $|10|_2 = |2 \cdot 5|_2 = 1/2$. Under the $5$-adic absolute value, the distance is $|10|_5 = |2 \cdot 5|_5 = 1/5$. The same symbol string yields three different "sizes" — $10$, $1/2$, $1/5$ — depending on which valuation the reader applies.

This observation generalizes. The central claim of this paper is:

> **The metric is not carried by the symbol. It is imposed by the reader.**

Number representation systems, from pre-numeric tally marks to modern positional notation, encode relational structure — correspondence, order, nesting — without committing to any particular valuation. The choice of metric (Archimedean vs. ultrametric) is a free parameter of the interpretative framework, not a property of the representation.

We call this claim **metric neutrality**, and we support it through three converging lines of evidence:

1. **Anthropological (Ifrah):** Georges Ifrah's encyclopedic *Universal History of Numbers* documents that number systems are fossilized forms of cultural cognitive choices. The decimal place-value system is not "natural" — it is a brilliant engineering solution that made algorithmic manipulation automatic at the cost of hiding the underlying relational structure. Pre-numeric primitives (tally sticks, pebbles, quipus) encode correspondence, order, and nesting without encoding any valuation.

2. **Mathematical (Ostrowski):** Ostrowski's theorem (1916) proves that every nontrivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to either the real absolute value or a $p$-adic absolute value for some prime $p$. There are exactly two families of metrics on the rational numbers. The Archimedean reading is not the default — it is one of two equally fundamental options. Any representation that encodes rational numbers is necessarily compatible with both families.

3. **Applied (QWAV):** The QWAV framework (Ultrametric Quantum Computing & AI) uses ultrametric structure — the strong triangle inequality, nested ball topology, hierarchical tree organization — for error confinement and quantum coherence. Metric neutrality provides the mathematical and anthropological foundation for this choice: the ultrametric reading is not a foreign imposition on classical number systems but a recovery of a reading that has always been latent.

The remainder of this paper develops these claims in detail, culminating in a formal axiomatic framework for metric-neutral representation theory.

## 2. Ifrah's Anthropology of Number Systems

Georges Ifrah's *Universal History of Numbers* (first published in French 1981, English translation 2000) is an encyclopedic survey of how human cultures have represented, counted, and manipulated numbers from prehistory to the computer age. Ifrah's core methodological commitment is that number systems are not mathematical inevitabilities but fossilized forms of particular cognitive and cultural choices [Ifrah, 2000].

### 2.1 The Contingency of Base Choice

Ifrah documents the sheer diversity of number bases used across human history: Babylonian sexagesimal (base 60), Mayan vigesimal (base 20), Egyptian and Roman decimal (base 10, additive only), and Indian positional decimal. No single base is "natural." Base choice reflects finger-counting habits (ten fingers), astronomical periods (Babylonian 60), or the availability of body-part counting methods (Mesoamerican 20). The global dominance of base 10 is a historical accident — a result of the transmission of Indian positional notation through the Islamic world to Europe, where its algorithmic superiority made it the standard for commerce and science.

Ifrah's crucial claim for our purposes is that **the decimal system was adopted without any active promotion by any agency** [Dauben, n.d., reviewing Ifrah]. It won because it worked well, not because it was mandated or because it corresponds to any deep mathematical truth. The same computational work could be — and historically was — done in other bases.

### 2.2 Pre-Numeric Primitives

Before the invention of abstract numerical notation, humans counted using physical marks and objects:

- **Tally sticks:** Marks on bone or wood, each notch corresponding to one counted item. The Ishango bone ($\sim$20,000 BCE) is the most famous example.
- **Body-part counting:** Finger counting (universal), with enormous cultural variation in which fingers, toes, joints, elbows, or other body parts were used and in what order.
- **Pebbles and tokens:** Small objects used for correspondence counting — one pebble per sheep, one token per unit of grain — found across Mesopotamia and the ancient Near East.
- **Knotted cords (quipus):** The Inca used complex knotted-string records encoding numerical and possibly narrative information through the position, color, and type of knots.

The critical observation about these systems is what they encode and what they do not. They encode:

- **Correspondence:** One physical mark per counted item.
- **Order:** The sequence of marks tracks the sequence of counted items.
- **Nesting:** Groups of groups, as in quipu hierarchies or body-part counting that proceeds through hierarchical levels (finger joints $\to$ fingers $\to$ hands $\to$ persons).

They do NOT encode:

- **Valuation:** A notch says "this is the $n$-th counted item." It does not say whether the distance from the first item is $n$ (Archimedean) or $p^{-\nu_p(n)}$ (ultrametric).

The pre-numeric primitive is **valuation-agnostic**. It carries the raw relational data on which a metric can later be imposed — but the metric itself is not in the marks.

### 2.3 Positional Notation as a Protocol

Ifrah locates the invention of positional (place-value) notation in India, with evidence pointing to at least 458 CE. The key innovations were nine distinct digit symbols, a place-value convention, and a zero symbol (śūnya) to mark an empty column.

Ifrah emphasizes that this was a genuine invention, not a discovery. The place-value convention is a **protocol** — a set of rules for reading symbol strings — and the rules must be learned and shared. The same symbol string can be read under different conventions (different bases), yielding different quantities. Extending this logic: the same string can be read under different valuations (Archimedean, $p$-adic), yielding different distances. The notation does not choose; the protocol does.

## 3. The $p$-adic Valuation and Ultrametric Distance

### 3.1 Definitions

Let $p$ be a prime number. The **$p$-adic valuation** $\nu_p(x)$ of a non-zero integer $x$ is the highest exponent $n$ such that $p^n$ divides $x$: $\nu_p(x) = \max\{n \in \mathbb{N}_0 : p^n \mid x\}$. For rational numbers $x = a/b$ (in lowest terms), $\nu_p(a/b) = \nu_p(a) - \nu_p(b)$, and $\nu_p(0) = \infty$.

The **$p$-adic absolute value** is $|x|_p = p^{-\nu_p(x)}$ (with $|0|_p = 0$). This is a non-Archimedean absolute value: numbers that are "highly divisible" by $p$ are $p$-adically small.

The $p$-adic distance is $d_p(x, y) = |x - y|_p$. It satisfies the **strong triangle inequality**:

$$d_p(x, z) \leq \max(d_p(x, y), d_p(y, z))$$

This is the defining property of an ultrametric space. In an ultrametric space, all triangles are isosceles, all points in a ball are centers, and intersecting balls are nested.

### 3.2 The Same String, Two Metrics

The opening example of this paper — the string `10` — can now be made precise:

| Valuation | $|10|$ | Distance type |
|:----------|:------|:--------------|
| Real ($|\cdot|$) | $10$ | Archimedean |
| $2$-adic ($|\cdot|_2$) | $1/2$ | Ultrametric |
| $5$-adic ($|\cdot|_5$) | $1/5$ | Ultrametric |
| $p$-adic, $p \neq 2, 5$ | $1$ | Ultrametric |

The string `10`, interpreted as the integer ten, admits four different "sizes" depending on which absolute value is applied. The notation does not — cannot — select among them. The selection is made by the interpretative framework.

More generally, for any positional string $d_n \cdots d_0$ in base $b$, the real value is $\sum d_i b^i$ (Archimedean), while the $p$-adic valuation is $\min\{i : d_i \neq 0\}$ (for prime $p$ compatible with the base). The same string, two fundamentally different readings.

## 4. Ostrowski's Theorem: Two Families, No Privilege

### 4.1 Statement

**Theorem (Ostrowski, 1916):** Every nontrivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to either the standard real absolute value $|\cdot|$ or a $p$-adic absolute value $|\cdot|_p$ for some prime $p$. These two families are mutually inequivalent.

**Corollary:** On $\mathbb{Q}$, there are exactly countably infinitely many inequivalent nontrivial absolute values: one Archimedean and one for each prime $p$.

### 4.2 Proof Sketch

The proof proceeds by case analysis. Let $|\cdot|$ be a nontrivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Case 1 (Archimedean):** There exists an integer $n > 1$ with $|n| > 1$. Let $n_0$ be the smallest such integer. Then one shows that $|m| = m^{\alpha}$ for all integers $m$, where $\alpha = \log_{n_0}|n_0| > 0$, establishing equivalence to the real absolute value.

**Case 2 (Non-Archimedean):** $|n| \leq 1$ for all integers $n > 0$. Since the absolute value is nontrivial, some integer has $|n| < 1$. Let $p$ be the smallest such integer. One shows that $p$ must be prime and that $|x| = |x|_p^{\alpha}$ for all $x \in \mathbb{Q}$, where $\alpha = -\log_p|p| > 0$, establishing equivalence to the $p$-adic absolute value.

### 4.3 Implications for Metric Neutrality

Ostrowski's theorem provides the mathematical foundation for metric neutrality:

1. **The Archimedean reading is not the default.** It is one of two equally fundamental families. There is no mathematical sense in which the real absolute value is "more natural" than the $p$-adic absolute values.

2. **The ultrametric reading is not exotic.** It is the other half of a complete classification. For every prime $p$, there is a $p$-adic absolute value that is as mathematically legitimate as the real one.

3. **Any representation of rational numbers is compatible with both families.** Because $\mathbb{Q}$ itself admits both, any notation that encodes elements of $\mathbb{Q}$ encodes elements that have both Archimedean and $p$-adic valuations. The representation does not select among them — the field structure guarantees that both are available.

4. **Metric neutrality is a theorem.** The claim that the same representation can be read under different metrics is not a philosophical stance. It is a consequence of Ostrowski's classification of absolute values on $\mathbb{Q}$, combined with the observation that standard number representations encode rational numbers.

## 5. Formal Framework: Metric-Neutral Representation Theory

### 5.1 Representation Systems

**Definition 1.** A **representation system** over $\mathbb{Q}$ is a triple $\mathcal{R} = (\Sigma, \pi, \iota)$ where:
- $\Sigma$ is a set of symbolic configurations.
- $\pi : \Sigma \to \mathbb{Q}$ is a parsing function.
- $\iota : \mathbb{Q} \to \Sigma$ is a generation function with $\pi \circ \iota = \text{id}_{\mathbb{Q}}$.

**Definition 2.** A representation system carries **relational primitives** if there exist a correspondence relation $\sim \subseteq \Sigma \times S$ (mapping configurations to counted objects) and a partial order $\preceq$ on $\Sigma$. A representation system is **purely relational** if it carries only relational primitives and no additional numerical structure.

**Examples:** The tally system ($\iota(n) = \underbrace{||\cdots|}_{n}$) is purely relational. Positional notation in base $b$ ($\pi(d_k \cdots d_0) = \sum d_i b^i$) is NOT purely relational — it adds grouping (the base) and positional weighting.

### 5.2 Valuations

**Definition 3.** A **valuation** on $\mathcal{R}$ is an absolute value $|\cdot| : \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ applied to parsed numbers. The distance between configurations $x, y \in \Sigma$ is $d(x, y) = |\pi(x) - \pi(y)|$. A valuation is **compatible** with $\mathcal{R}$ if it is an absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Definition 4.** A representation system $\mathcal{R}$ is **metric-neutral** if there exist at least two inequivalent compatible valuations.

### 5.3 Main Results

**Theorem 1 (Tally System Neutrality).** The tally representation system is metric-neutral: it admits both the real absolute value and any $p$-adic absolute value as compatible valuations.

**Theorem 2 (Positional Notation Neutrality).** Positional notation in any base $b \geq 2$ is metric-neutral: it admits both the real absolute value and any $p$-adic absolute value.

**Theorem 3 (Ostrowski Completeness).** For any representation system with $\pi(\Sigma) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$, the set of compatible nontrivial valuations is a (non-empty) subset of the two Ostrowski families. No representation system can introduce a valuation not equivalent to the real absolute value or some $p$-adic absolute value.

### 5.4 The Metric-Neutrality Hierarchy

| Level | Description | Example | Compatible Valuations |
|:------|:------------|:--------|:----------------------|
| **Level 0: Purely Relational** | Encodes correspondence, order, nesting. No numerical abstraction. | Tally stick, pebble count | All Ostrowski families |
| **Level 1: Grouped but Unvalued** | Adds grouping convention (base) but does not privilege any valuation. | Positional notation | All Ostrowski families |
| **Level 2: Valued** | Explicitly encodes or privileges a specific valuation. | Vector with magnitude | Restricted to one family |
| **Level 3: Metric-Complete** | Fully specifies a metric completion. | $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{Q}_p$ | Exactly one valuation |

**Theorem 4 (Principle of Metric Neutrality).** For all representation systems at Level 0 or Level 1, the choice of which valuation to apply is a free parameter of the interpretative framework — it is not determined by the representation.

## 6. QWAV Integration: Pre-Numeric Primitives as Ultrametric Data Structures

### 6.1 The QWAV Framework

The QWAV framework (Ultrametric Quantum Computing & AI) uses ultrametric structure as the foundation for quantum information processing. Its key principles are:

- **Error confinement:** The strong triangle inequality $|x + y|_p \leq \max(|x|_p, |y|_p)$ bounds combined errors by the maximum of individual errors — errors do not accumulate additively.
- **Nested ball topology:** In an ultrametric space, overlapping balls are nested, enabling hierarchical information organization.
- **Tree-like structure:** Ultrametric spaces are isomorphic to trees, providing natural hierarchical algorithms.

### 6.2 Metric Neutrality as QWAV's Foundation

Metric neutrality provides the missing foundation for QWAV:

1. **Mathematical legitimacy:** Ostrowski's theorem proves that the choice of $p$-adic valuation is a selection from the valuations that $\mathbb{Q}$ itself admits. QWAV is not imposing an exotic structure — it is recovering a reading that was always available.

2. **Historical depth:** Ifrah's documentation shows that hierarchical, tree-like number representations (tally sticks, quipus) are at least as old as linear-magnitude representations. The ultrametric reading is not a 20th-century invention — it is a recovery of structure latent in the earliest human counting systems.

3. **Physical instantiation:** Pre-numeric primitives are physically realized ultrametric data structures. A tally stick, read $p$-adically, is a tree: notches are leaves, and the distance between them is determined by their lowest common ancestor in the divisibility tree. A quipu is an explicitly hierarchical representation, with its cord-and-knot structure mapping directly to a tree topology.

### 6.3 New Research Directions

The metric-neutrality framework suggests several new directions for QWAV:

- **Pre-numeric QWAV primitives:** Computational primitives based directly on tally sticks, quipus, or hierarchical body-part counting — native to ultrametric space from the start.
- **Multi-valuation quantum theory:** A quantum theory using the adele ring $\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}$ as its state space, working across all completions simultaneously.
- **Cognitive QWAV:** Exploiting the apparent human capacity for hierarchical numerical thinking for human-quantum interfaces.

## 7. Composite Bases

The $p$-adic valuation is defined for prime $p$, but human number systems overwhelmingly use composite bases: 10, 60, 20. Metric neutrality extends naturally via the Chinese Remainder Theorem.

For a composite base $b = \prod p_i^{e_i}$, the positional expansion simultaneously encodes $p_i$-adic information for each prime factor. The **product valuation**:

$$\nu_b(x) = \min_i \left\lfloor \frac{\nu_{p_i}(x)}{e_i} \right\rfloor$$

counts the number of trailing zeros in base $b$ and provides a natural composite-base ultrametric. The corresponding absolute value $|x|_b = b^{-\nu_b(x)}$ adds a third family of compatible valuations for composite bases.

Historical composite bases gain new significance under this reading: Babylonian sexagesimal (60) simultaneously encodes $2$-adic, $3$-adic, and $5$-adic structure; Mayan vigesimal (20) encodes $2$-adic and $5$-adic structure; global decimal (10) encodes $2$-adic and $5$-adic structure. Trailing zeros — the very feature that makes numbers "round" — are precisely what determines their $p$-adic closeness to zero.

## 8. Educational Implications

Standard mathematics education presents the Archimedean valuation as the only meaningful way to assign a "size" to a number. If metric neutrality is correct, this education is incomplete. We propose a **metric-neutral module** at four educational stages:

1. **Elementary (grades 4–6):** "Ways to Measure" — introducing the idea that the same tally stick can be measured in different ways.
2. **High school (grades 9–10):** "Two Triangle Inequalities" — the standard inequality alongside the strong inequality, using modular arithmetic as motivation.
3. **Undergraduate:** "Two Completions of $\mathbb{Q}$" — presenting $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p$ as parallel completions, with Ostrowski's theorem as the capstone.
4. **Graduate:** "Metric-Neutral Mathematics" — the adelic perspective, non-Archimedean analysis, and metric-neutral computation.

Pedagogical advantages include teaching the "hidden assumption" skill (the Archimedean valuation is an assumption, not a fact), the "multiple valid answers" intuition, and connections to computer science (binary as $2$-adic base).

## 9. Cognitive Anthropology

If representations are metric-neutral, how do humans actually read them? The cognitive evidence suggests that the Archimedean number line is **learned, not innate**:

- Young children map numbers logarithmically (compressing larger numbers), transitioning to the linear/Archimedean mapping only around age 7–8, correlated with mathematical achievement.
- The SNARC effect (spatial-numerical association) is culturally specific — it reverses in right-to-left reading cultures.
- Amazonian peoples with limited number vocabularies use logarithmic rather than linear mappings, suggesting the logarithmic mapping is the default and the linear mapping is culturally acquired.

These findings suggest that the human cognitive architecture for number is pre-metric (or at least not inherently Archimedean). We are born metric-neutral; we are taught to be Archimedean; and we could, in principle, be taught to be $p$-adic as well. The metric-neutrality thesis makes testable predictions: training on $p$-adic comparison should not transfer to Archimedean comparison (and vice versa), the classical distance effect should reverse under $p$-adic instruction, and the intraparietal sulcus (IPS) should show distinct activation patterns for the two valuation types.

## 10. Conclusion

We have argued that number representation systems are metric-neutral: they encode relational structure (correspondence, order, nesting) without committing to any particular valuation. The metric — Archimedean or ultrametric — is imposed by the reader, not carried by the symbol.

This thesis rests on three converging foundations:

- **Anthropological:** Ifrah's documentation of the diversity of number systems shows that base choice, grouping conventions, and notational form are historically contingent — cultural choices, not mathematical necessities.
- **Mathematical:** Ostrowski's theorem proves that $\mathbb{Q}$ admits exactly two families of nontrivial absolute values, establishing that the Archimedean-$p$-adic duality is a theorem about the field structure itself, not an artifact of any particular notation.
- **Formal:** The axiomatic framework of representation systems, relational primitives, compatible valuations, and the metric-neutrality hierarchy provides a precise language for analyzing when and how representations commit to — or remain neutral with respect to — metrics.

The framework has concrete implications for quantum computing (QWAV's ultrametric architecture is mathematically legitimate, not exotic), mathematics education (students should learn that the Archimedean valuation is a choice), and cognitive science (the human number sense may be genuinely pre-metric, with the Archimedean reading being a learned overlay).

The core insight is simple but far-reaching: the string `10` does not know whether it's Archimedean or $p$-adic. We do. And we can choose.
